What does NVIDIA do?

NVIDIA is a semiconductor company in the technology sector that operates as a data center scale AI infrastructure company in the United States, Taiwan, China, Hong Kong, Europe and internationally. It is headquartered in Santa Clara, US, and has 42,000 full-time employees. Its business is divided into two segments: Compute & Networking, and Graphics.

The Compute & Networking segment provides data center accelerated computing and networking platforms, and artificial intelligence solutions and software. It also runs automotive platforms, including autonomous and electric vehicle solutions and related software. The Graphics segment supplies GeForce GPUs for gaming and PCs, and Quadro and RTX GPUs for enterprise workstation graphics.

NVIDIA's products are used in gaming, professional visualization, data center and automotive markets. Its customers include original equipment manufacturers (OEMs), original device manufacturers (ODMs), system integrators, independent software vendors, cloud service providers, automotive manufacturers and tier-1 automotive suppliers.

On partnerships, it is working with Cisco to develop USFP, a high-density optical module transceiver. It is also working with ChipAgents to develop Build-A-Chip-Bench, a benchmark for AI agents carrying out chip design tasks, and has a strategic alliance with MediaTek to build the next generations of AI computing platforms.

How have its annual results and revenue changed?

NVIDIA's annual revenue grew 700.5% from $27.0 billion in 2023 to $215.9 billion in 2026, an average growth rate of 100.0% a year. Operating income and net income also expanded sharply over the same period.

In 2023, it posted revenue of $27.0 billion, operating income of $5.6 billion (operating margin 20.7%) and net income of $4.4 billion. In 2024, revenue rose to $60.9 billion, with operating income of $33.0 billion (operating margin 54.1%) and net income of $29.8 billion.

In 2025, revenue reached $130.5 billion, operating income $81.5 billion (operating margin 62.4%) and net income $72.9 billion. In 2026, it earned operating income of $130.4 billion (operating margin 60.4%) and net income of $120.1 billion, continuing its high profitability.

How has its price swung, and what does it pay in dividends?

Based on daily closing prices over the past 10 years, NVIDIA's maximum drawdown (MDD: the percentage fall from the highest value to the lowest value) was -66.3%. The fall began at the peak on November 29, 2021 and took 319 days to reach the bottom on October 14, 2022.

It then took 223 days from the bottom, until May 25, 2023, to get back to the previous peak price. Had KRW 10 million been invested at the November 2021 peak, its value at the lowest price would have shrunk to KRW 3.37 million. The share price is currently -47.5% below its 10-year high.

On dividends, the annual dividend per share rose from $0.016 in 2020 to $0.040 in 2025, an average increase of +20.1% a year over 5 years. The dividend per share stayed at $0.016 each year from 2020 to 2023, then came to $0.034 in 2024 and $0.040 in 2025. The annual dividend yield at the current price is 0.03%.

How much of NVIDIA is in major ETFs?
