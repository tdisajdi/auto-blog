What does Tesla do, and how have its results been?

Tesla (Tesla, Inc., ticker TSLA) is a US-listed company headquartered in the United States, in the Auto Manufacturers industry within the Consumer Cyclical sector. It has 134,785 full-time employees.

Its business is split into two segments: Automotive, and Energy Generation and Storage. It designs, makes, sells and leases electric vehicles in the US, China and global markets, and runs businesses in regulatory credit sales, non-warranty maintenance services, collision repair, auto insurance, parts sales and accessories. Its vehicles are offered through direct sales and used car trading, the Supercharger network, in-app upgrades, purchase financing and leasing, and its own service centers and mobile technicians.

In the energy segment, it designs, makes, installs, sells and leases solar energy generation and energy storage products for residential, commercial and industrial customers and utilities. It supplies the lithium-ion battery storage products Powerwall and Megapack, along with energy generation products such as solar panels and Solar Roof, together with warranty services and a range of financing options.

From 2022 to 2025, Tesla's annual revenue grew from $81.5 billion to $94.8 billion, a total increase of 16.4%, or 5.2% a year on average. Revenue rose to $97.7 billion in 2024 before falling to $94.8 billion in 2025, and profitability fell. Operating profit dropped from $13.8 billion in 2022 to $4.8 billion in 2025, and the operating margin fell from 17.0% to 5.1%. Net income peaked at $15.0 billion in 2023 and was $3.8 billion in 2025.

How far did it fall when the price swung?

How hard the price swung can be seen from the maximum drawdown (MDD, the percentage fall from the highest value to the lowest value, i.e. the size of the biggest loss over that period).

Based on daily closing prices over the past 10 years, Tesla's maximum drawdown was -73.6%. The fall from the peak on November 4, 2021 to the low on January 3, 2023 lasted 425 days in total. Had you bought 10 million won worth at the peak, its value would have fallen to 2.64 million won at the lowest price.

After this fall, regaining the high took more time from the day of the bottom. It was not until December 11, 2024, 708 days after the low, that the price got back to its previous peak. As of November 22, 2026, the share price is -29.4% below the highest price of the past 10 years.

How much Tesla do you get through an ETF?

Tesla's weight is 1.73% in SPY, which tracks the S&P 500, 3.41% in QQQ, which follows the Nasdaq-100, and 18.78% in XLY, a consumer discretionary sector ETF.

If you get exposure through major US-listed ETFs instead of buying Tesla shares directly, each fund gives it a different weight depending on how it is built and what it targets.

Put 1 million won into SPY and about 20,000 won of it goes to Tesla. In QQQ, about 30,000 won of a 1 million won investment goes to the stock, and buying 1 million won of XLY, which concentrates on the consumer discretionary sector, puts about 190,000 won into Tesla. Weights inside an ETF shift a little every day with each stock's price moves and index rebalancing.

Summary
