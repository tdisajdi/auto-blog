Over the past 5 years, starting November 12, 2021, QQQ (Invesco QQQ Trust), which tracks the underlying index, rose 58.8%, while the actual return of TQQQ (ProShares UltraPro QQQ), a leveraged ETF that aims for 3 times the daily return, was only 4.7%. Over the same period, QQQ's maximum drawdown (MDD: the percentage fall from the highest value to the lowest value, the largest loss over the period) was -35.54%, while TQQQ's widened to -81.36%. These returns are in dollars, so exchange-rate changes are not included, and the longer the holding period, the further the actual result drifted from a simple calculation.

Why does the result drift from 3 times the index over time?

A leveraged ETF resets every day to target 3 times the underlying index's daily return, so when prices go up and down repeatedly, the result differs even for the same underlying return. QQQ's annual volatility, calculated from its daily returns, was 22.4% over the past year, 21.3% over 3 years and 23.5% over 5 years. In a market with this kind of volatility, resetting the target ratio every day creates a gap in returns.

A simple calculation shows the principle. Suppose the underlying index rises +10.0% one day and falls -9.09% the next, returning to its starting price. An ETF that tracks 3 times the daily move rises +30.0% on the first day, then falls -27.27% (3 times the drop) on the second, ending with a -5.45% loss instead of returning to where it started. The bigger the volatility, the wider the gap between 3 times the underlying return and the ETF's actual result.

The gap in actual returns over 1, 3 and 5 years

Over the past year, starting November 19, 2025, QQQ rose +1.9% and the simple 'QQQ × 3' calculation came to +5.7%, but TQQQ's actual return was -17.0%, a gap of -22.7%p (%p: the difference between two rates, which is not the same as a percentage increase). Over the past 3 years, starting November 15, 2023, QQQ rose +60.5% while TQQQ actually returned +97.7%. Compared with the simple calculation of +181.4% for that period, the gap was -83.7%p.

Over the past 5 years, QQQ rose +58.8% and the simple calculation reached +176.5%, but TQQQ's actual return stayed at +4.7%, the largest gap at -171.8%p. In a period of steady rises in one direction the ETF can climb even higher, but as prices swing up and down over longer periods, the gap between the simple calculation and the actual account return widens.

Drawdowns in falling markets

Over the past 5 years, TQQQ's maximum drawdown was -81.36%, far deeper than QQQ's -35.54% over the same period. After a fall this large, the gain needed to get back to the original amount becomes disproportionately large.

In Korea, trading gains on US-listed ETFs are subject to capital gains tax, which investors report themselves in May of the following year.

Frequently asked questions

🔸 Can US-listed leveraged ETFs such as TQQQ be bought in an ISA or pension savings account?

Overseas-listed ETFs (such as those listed in the US) cannot be bought directly in Korea's ISA, pension savings or IRP accounts. The tax benefits of those accounts are available through Korea-listed ETFs that track the same index.

Summary

A leveraged ETF that targets 3 times the daily return resets every day, so when held for a long time, a large gap can open between the simple calculation and the actual return, depending on market volatility. The more prices go up and down, the more this leakage accumulates, and the result can fall short of even the underlying index.

There are three things you can check yourself when looking at a leveraged ETF. First, how much the underlying index swung (QQQ's 5-year annual volatility: 23.5%). Second, how much it shrank at its biggest fall (TQQQ's 5-year maximum drawdown: -81.36%). Third, how far 'underlying return × 3' and the actual return drifted apart as the period lengthened (over 5 years, QQQ +58.8% while TQQQ returned +4.7%, a gap of -171.8%p).

In one line: because a leveraged ETF resets to 3 times the daily return every day, the longer you hold it, the more its return can fall below the simple calculation.
