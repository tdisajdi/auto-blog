What does Samsung Electronics, a 1,233 trillion won company, actually do?

Samsung Electronics is a listed Korean company headquartered in South Korea. It belongs to the Technology sector and the Consumer Electronics industry. It runs information technology, mobile communications, home appliance and device solutions businesses around the world, and its market value reaches 1,233.4 trillion won.

Its main operations are split into four segments. Through four segments, DX, DS, SDC and Harman, Samsung Electronics supplies a wide portfolio of products and services worldwide, from smartphones to semiconductors and automotive electronics.

🔸 Four segments and main products

  • Segments: Device eXperience (DX), Device Solutions (DS), SDC and Harman.
  • Devices: smartphones, tablets, audio and sound products, watches, switches, accessories, TVs, sound devices, home appliances such as refrigerators, washers and dryers, vacuum cleaners, cooking appliances, dishwashers, air conditioners and air purifiers, and monitors.
  • Components and screens: memory storage, displays, smart and LED signage, contract chip manufacturing (foundry) and System LSI.
  • Cars and businesses: connected car systems, audio and visual products, enterprise automation solutions and connected services.
  • Other services and investments: venture capital investment, cloud services, network equipment installation, digital advertising platforms, logistics, finance, software design, and development of defect and quality control software.

How did its results move from 2022 to 2025?

Once you have a sense of how big the company is, a useful yardstick for its results is how far it recovered from the year its operating margin was lowest. Samsung Electronics' annual income statements for fiscal years 2022 to 2025 show clearly how its results changed with the business cycle.

The low point in these four years was 2023. That year operating profit fell to 6.6 trillion won and the operating margin dropped to 2.5%, but in 2025 it recorded revenue of 333.6 trillion won and operating profit of 43.6 trillion won, recovering to an operating margin of 13.1%. Samsung Electronics' annual revenue rose from 302.2 trillion won in 2022 to 333.6 trillion won in 2025, an increase of 10.4%, or 3.3% a year on average.

A 50% fall from its 10-year high: what the drawdown and dividends really look like

When measuring how much a stock's price swings, it is important to check the maximum drawdown (MDD: the percentage fall from the highest value to the lowest value, i.e. the size of the biggest loss over that period). Here is the result calculated from daily closing prices over the past 10 years. After peaking on June 18, 2026, Samsung Electronics fell for 158 days to a bottom on November 23, 2026, a maximum drawdown of -50.1%.

Had you invested 10 million won at the peak, when the price was highest, its value at the lowest price would have shrunk to 4.99 million won. Since that fall, the share price has not yet regained its peak.

Separately from how far the share price fell, the dividend record is also worth checking. The annual dividend per share paid each year was as follows.

  • 2020: 2,994 won
  • 2021: 1,444 won
  • 2022: 1,444 won
  • 2023: 1,444 won
  • 2024: 1,446 won
  • 2025: 1,668 won

The five-year average annual change is -11.0%, but the 2020 dividend was more than 1.5 times the next year's, so this number alone does not mean the dividend kept shrinking. The dividend yield at the current price is 0.94%.

Summary

Through its four core segments, Samsung Electronics recovered to revenue in the 300 trillion won range and operating profit in the 40 trillion won range, yet its share price fell sharply in 2026. Its figures show that a recovery in results and the share price do not necessarily move in the same direction.

One-line summary: Samsung Electronics' share price fell by half in 158 days, regardless of its recovery in results.
