A 1,192 trillion won company: what Samsung Electronics does

Samsung Electronics (Samsung Electronics Co., Ltd.) is a Korean company that runs information technology, mobile communications, home appliance and component businesses in global markets. According to the Yahoo Finance company profile, it operates in four segments: Device eXperience (DX), Device Solutions (DS), SDC and Harman.

🔸 Main products and businesses

Its consumer products include smartphones, tablets, audio products, watches and accessories, as well as appliances such as TVs, sound devices, refrigerators, washing machines, dryers, vacuum cleaners, cooking appliances, dishwashers, air conditioners and air purifiers. Monitors, smart and LED signage, connected car systems and automation solutions for businesses are also on its list of businesses.

On the component side, it makes memory storage products, display panels, foundry services and System LSI, and it also does contract processing and sales of semiconductors and display panels, and develops software for semiconductor process defect and quality control.

Beyond this, it offers audio and video products and connected services, and is also active in venture capital investment, cloud services, network equipment installation, digital advertising platforms, logistics, finance and software design. Through its four segments, Samsung Electronics supplies products and services to the global technology market, from finished goods such as smartphones and appliances to components such as memory chips, displays and automotive electronics.

Revenue above 330 trillion won: how annual results moved

Samsung Electronics' annual income statements show how its size and profitability changed from 2022 to 2025. Annual revenue, 302.2 trillion won in 2022, rose to 333.6 trillion won in 2025, growth of 10.4% over three years, or 3.3% a year on average.

🔸 Profitability: the low point and the recovery

Unlike revenue, operating profit and net income fell sharply in 2023. Operating profit dropped from 43.4 trillion won in 2022 to 6.6 trillion won in 2023, and the operating margin fell from 14.4% to 2.5%. Net income over the same period fell from 54.7 trillion won in 2022 to 14.5 trillion won in 2023.

Results then recovered, with revenue of 300.9 trillion won, operating profit of 32.7 trillion won (an operating margin of 10.9%) and net income of 33.6 trillion won in 2024. In 2025, with revenue of 333.6 trillion won, it earned operating profit of 43.6 trillion won (an operating margin of 13.1%) and net income of 44.3 trillion won, back to its 2022 level of profitability.

A 48.6% maximum drawdown: the record of the fall

In stock investing, the maximum drawdown (MDD: the percentage fall from the highest value to the lowest value, i.e. the size of the biggest loss over that period) means the largest loss you could have experienced while holding an asset. Based on daily closing prices over the past 10 years, Samsung Electronics' most recent maximum drawdown was -48.6%.

🔸 How long the fall lasted and what it did to an investment

This fall began after the peak on June 18, 2026 and lasted 155 days, until the bottom on November 20, 2026. The current price is -48.6% below its 10-year high, and it has not yet regained its peak price.

Converting it into an actual amount makes the size of the move clear. Had you invested 10 million won at the peak on June 18, 2026, its value would have shrunk to 5.14 million won at the lowest price 155 days later, on November 20, 2026. Shares bought at the very top lost close to half their value through the fall.

Dividends per share and the current yield

The annual dividend per share was 2,994 won in 2020 and 1,668 won in 2025. Because these figures compare only two years, they do not show the year-by-year path in between.

🔸 The yield at the current price and how it is calculated

Calculated at the current share price, Samsung Electronics' dividend yield is about 0.91%. This yield changes as the share price moves.
