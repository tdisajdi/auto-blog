Alphabet (Alphabet Inc., ticker GOOGL), with a market value of $3.26 trillion (about 4,353 trillion won) as of November 22, 2026, is a US-listed company that recorded annual revenue of $402.8 billion in 2025. Its maximum drawdown (MDD: the percentage fall from the highest value to the lowest value), the largest fall from a past peak, was -44.3%, and its weight in ETFs includes 11.08% in XLC and 2.51% in SPY.

Alphabet belongs to the Internet Content & Information industry within the Communication Services sector and runs several business segments in global markets. Besides direct investment in the stock, it is a major holding in leading US index ETFs and sector ETFs.

This post lays out, from public figures, Alphabet's three core business segments, its annual results from 2022 to 2025, its record of price falls from past highs, and its weight in major ETFs.

What are Alphabet's segments, and how have its annual results moved?

Founded in 1998 and headquartered in Mountain View, California, Alphabet offers a range of products and platforms in the United States, Europe, the Middle East, Africa, the Asia-Pacific, Canada and Latin America, and has 204,757 full-time employees. Its business is split into three segments: Google Services, Google Cloud and Other Bets.

Google Services includes ads, Android, Chrome, device sales, Gmail, Google Drive, Google Maps, Google Photos, Google Play, Search and YouTube. It also covers sales of apps and digital content in Google Play and YouTube, and subscription services such as YouTube TV, YouTube Music and Premium, NFL Sunday Ticket and Google One. Google Cloud offers consumption-based fees and subscriptions for AI solutions such as AI infrastructure, the Vertex AI platform and Gemini enterprise, as well as cybersecurity, data analytics, Google Workspace for businesses (Calendar, Gmail, Docs, Drive, Meet and more) and other enterprise services. Other Bets sells transportation and internet services.

🔸 Annual results

From 2022 to 2025, Alphabet's annual revenue grew from $282.8 billion to $402.8 billion, up +42.4% (+12.5% a year on average). In 2022 it recorded revenue of $282.8 billion, operating profit of $74.8 billion (an operating margin of 26.5%) and net income of $60.0 billion. The following year, 2023, these rose to revenue of $307.4 billion, operating profit of $84.3 billion (an operating margin of 27.4%) and net income of $73.8 billion.

In 2024 it recorded revenue of $350.0 billion, operating profit of $112.4 billion (an operating margin of 32.1%) and net income of $100.1 billion. In 2025 it reached revenue of $402.8 billion, operating profit of $129.0 billion (an operating margin of 32.0%) and net income of $132.2 billion.

How far has the share price fallen in the past?

In the stock market, the maximum drawdown is the percentage fall from the highest price to the lowest price over a given period, showing the largest loss an investor could have faced during that time.

Alphabet's 10-year maximum drawdown was -44.3%: after peaking on November 18, 2021, it took 350 days to reach the bottom on November 3, 2022. Had you invested 10 million won at the 2021 peak, its value would have fallen to about 5.57 million won at the lowest point of the slump.

After the fall ended, it was not until January 25, 2024, 448 days after the bottom, that the price got back to its previous peak. As of November 22, 2026, Alphabet's share price is -34.0% below its 10-year high.

How much Alphabet is in major ETFs?

Alphabet is held in leading ETFs that track major US indexes, with different weights depending on each fund's nature.

In XLC, a leading US communication services sector ETF, Alphabet's weight is 11.08%, higher than in broad market index ETFs. Put 1 million won into this ETF and about 110,000 won of it goes to Alphabet shares.

In SPY (State Street SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust), which tracks the S&P 500, Alphabet's weight is 2.51%, so about 30,000 won of a 1 million won investment goes to Alphabet. In QQQ (Invesco QQQ Trust), which follows the Nasdaq-100, it makes up 2.66%, or about 30,000 won per 1 million won.

Weights inside an ETF shift a little every day with index rebalancing and each stock's daily price moves. Buying Alphabet shares directly exposes 100% of the money to that one stock, while buying through an ETF spreads the rest across the other holdings.

FAQ

🔸 What is Google's official company name and ticker?

The official company name is Alphabet (Alphabet Inc.), and its ticker on the US stock market is GOOGL. It is often called Google after its services, but its shares trade and its results are reported under the holding company's name, Alphabet.

🔸 What business segments does Alphabet run?

Alphabet runs its business through three segments: Google Services, Google Cloud and Other Bets. Google Services includes search, ads, YouTube, Android and hardware; Google Cloud provides AI solutions, infrastructure and Google Workspace; and Other Bets is made up of transportation and internet services.
