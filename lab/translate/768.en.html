What does Apple do, and how have its results changed?

Apple's revenue grew from $383.3 billion in 2023 to $478.8 billion in 2026, an average of +7.7% a year. Headquartered in the United States, the company is classified in the technology sector under consumer electronics and has 167,000 full-time employees. Its market capitalization was $5.09 trillion (about KRW 6,810 trillion) on the day the data was collected.

Its business designs, manufactures and sells the iPhone line of smartphones, the Mac line of personal computers and the iPad line of multi-purpose tablets. It also offers wearables, home products and accessories, including Apple Watch, AirPods, Beats products, Apple Vision Pro, Apple TV 4K and HomePod.

Beyond devices, it runs a range of services: advertising services, including third-party licensing arrangements and its own advertising platforms; AppleCare and cloud services; and payment services such as Apple Card and Apple Pay. It operates the App Store, where customers can download applications and digital content.

Its subscription services include Apple Arcade, a game service; Apple Fitness+, a personalized fitness service; Apple Music; Apple News+, a news service; and Apple TV, which offers original content. Its products and services are sold through its retail and online stores, its direct sales force, cellular network carriers and other resellers to consumer, small and mid-sized business, education, enterprise and government markets.

In 2023, Apple recorded revenue of $383.3 billion, operating income of $114.3 billion (operating margin 29.8%) and net income of $97.0 billion. In 2024, revenue was $391.0 billion, operating income $123.2 billion (operating margin 31.5%) and net income $93.7 billion.

In 2025, revenue rose to $416.2 billion, with operating income of $133.0 billion (operating margin 32.0%) and net income of $112.0 billion. In 2026, revenue was $478.8 billion, operating income $158.1 billion (operating margin 33.0%) and net income $132.2 billion, so revenue was 24.9% higher than in 2023.

How have its price swings and dividends moved?

The largest fall in the past 10 years, the maximum drawdown, was -38.5%. It ran for 92 days, from the peak on October 3, 2018 to the bottom on January 3, 2019. Had KRW 10 million been invested at that peak, its value at the lowest point would have shrunk to KRW 6.15 million.

The share price got back to its previous peak on October 10, 2019, 280 days after the bottom. Apple's share price is now -1.8% below its 10-year high.

On shareholder returns, the annual dividend per share rose from $0.81 in 2020 to $1.03 in 2025. Over those 5 years, the dividend grew by an average of +5.0% a year. The dividend yield at the current price is 0.29%.

How much of Apple is in major ETFs?

Representative index and sector ETFs hold Apple at different weights depending on what they track. SPY, which follows the S&P 500 index of large US companies, holds 7.70%; QQQ, which follows the Nasdaq-100 index, holds 8.16%; and XLK, which holds only the technology sector, holds 15.31%.

These weights change a little every day with relative price moves and regular rebalancing. Buying Apple shares directly puts 100% of the money into that one company; buying through an ETF spreads the rest of the money across the other stocks the ETF holds.
